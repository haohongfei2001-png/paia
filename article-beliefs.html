Not every input
is a belief.

Quotations, experiments, questions and changed minds need room of their own.

In this note

What we send AI is often more complicated than a settled opinion. Sometimes we are quoting another person. Sometimes we are trying to give a vague thought its first words. Keeping these expressions begins with respecting that difference.

An input can hold several voices.

A draft to rewrite, a job description or an argument you disagree with can all go into the composer. A record establishes that you sent those words. It does not establish that every sentence is your lasting position.

A question is not a conclusion either. ‘Could this work better?’ is an exploration. Organizing it as ‘I believe this is better’ quietly crosses the boundary of what was said.

Clarity should keep the conditions.

Organization can change order, hierarchy and detail to make a line of thought easier to read. It also needs to retain the less tidy but important parts: perhaps, for now, if, I am not certain yet.

In PAIA’s product principles, AI organization is a derived reading view over the material. Original expression, sources, perspective and uncertainty need to remain intact. An AI inference should not be presented as your belief. Live AI organization is still in development.

Past expression is material for the present.

Judgment changes with experience and circumstances. An earlier expression can remain while you continue to revise your working text, topic organization and AI access choices.

For an archive to serve a person, it needs to accommodate change. Remembering more should not make it harder for someone to be who they are now.
