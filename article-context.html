Where does your thinking go
when the chat ends?

Keeping the words also means keeping the ability to find, understand and use them again.

A conversation ends, but a question may continue for a long time. Across different windows, you explain the background, try approaches and rule things out. These expressions serve the task at hand and become part of your own body of thought.

You need to find it to use it again.

Sometimes you remember a conclusion but not the discussion it came from. Asking AI again may produce another answer, without recovering the constraints, concerns and judgment you brought to the earlier conversation.

Input Archive starts from sent personal inputs, retaining sources and providing retrieval and working-text editing. It gives you a way back to the particular sentence.

A conversation is a moment. A topic is a process.

A project can involve many conversations: one about the people it serves, another about narrowing scope, and another about admitting an earlier assumption did not hold. Rearranging conversations alone does not explain how those expressions relate.

Thought Library uses topics, sections and entries to create a continuous reading space. Your names, order and edits take priority, making room for accumulated thinking and unresolved questions alike.

Keep it for yourself. Choose what AI may see.

A personal space can serve your own reflection and use first. When an external AI needs background, it can read the corresponding material through explicit permission.

AI Context manages My Information, My Rules, My Now and My Inputs separately. Main access, cards and new topics start closed; live connections are still in development. Keeping content does not automatically open the whole archive.
